# Instructor - Airway Planning Lab

## Objective

Students practice recognizing a potentially difficult airway before induction and working through a backup plan when the first approach fails.

The goals for this lab are to have the student prepare for and manage a difficult airway through the following:

- Completing a focused airway exam
- Agreeing a primary and backup plan with the attending and surgeon
- Checking that backup equipment is at the bedside before induction
- Switching techniques when the first approach fails
- Describing a safe emergence plan

## Equipment

- Simulator running the baseline program
- Endotracheal tubes in two sizes, blades and handles
- Bougie
- Supraglottic airway
- Video laryngoscope
- Flexible scope

## Activity

This lab centers on preparation, planning and execution when the airway turns out to be harder than expected. The simulator's airway occluder is used to make mask ventilation or laryngoscopy difficult on cue.

## Instructor Pre-brief:

Brief both scenarios before starting. Set the scene each time: is the patient just arriving, or is the case already under way? Keep the patient history short; the exercise is about airway planning. The instructor plays the patient so students can perform a real airway exam.

## Scenario 1 - Knee Arthroscopy

**Patient** – A patient with long-standing rheumatoid arthritis presents for an elective knee arthroscopy. She has limited neck mobility and reports neck pain with extension.

### Goals:

- The team performs a focused airway exam and asks about range of motion
- The team asks for prior anesthetic records and the surgeon's view on positioning
- Students state that the head stays neutral during laryngoscopy and set up in-line stabilization
- After induction, alternatives such as bougie, video laryngoscope or flexible scope are considered
- The team agrees on a plan and secures the airway
- Once the airway is secured, end scenario

### Induction/Intubation –

Let the students gather airway information. The surgeon, when asked, says an asleep intubation is acceptable. Whatever the students plan, the attending suggests the airway looks straightforward and direct laryngoscopy is fine.

- If the students ask for the video laryngoscope, say it is being fetched but induction can proceed with in-line stabilization
- Mask ventilation works but laryngoscopy gives no view (switch on the airway occluder and hold the simulator's mouth partly closed)
- Ask the team what they want to do next
- This is the non-emergency pathway, so mask ventilation should continue between attempts
- Once alternatives have been discussed, the video laryngoscope arrives and the students may intubate
- Once intubation is confirmed, end scenario

## Scenario 2 – Submandibular Abscess Drainage

**Patient** – A patient presents for incision and drainage of a submandibular abscess. He has visible swelling under the jaw and limited mouth opening.

### Set up for this scenario:

- Tape a folded towel under the simulator's jaw to represent the swelling
- Sit the simulator up; the patient becomes short of breath when lying flat
- Video laryngoscope

### Activity

The students should choose an awake technique to secure the airway. If they do, say the attempt was unsuccessful and the case is cancelled, then rerun the scenario with an asleep induction. If the initial plan was an asleep induction, note it on the rubric.

### Goals:

- The team gathers more airway information: inspection, palpation of the swelling, imaging reports, whether the patient can lie flat
- The plan includes how the patient will be positioned for intubation
- Help is called at an appropriate time
- More than one mask-ventilation technique is tried, such as an oral airway, a nasal airway or two-handed ventilation

### Induction/Intubation -

Run the emergency non-invasive pathway of the algorithm. Once the students have induced, make the simulator difficult to mask-ventilate and difficult to intubate. Saturation falls. Cannot intubate, cannot ventilate. A supraglottic airway is next; say that ventilation through it is inadequate at first. Students should call for help and name the next step as a possible invasive airway. At that point ventilation through the supraglottic airway becomes adequate.

### Emergence/Extubation

Ask the students what they want to do next. The expected answer is to wake the patient. Students should state that the patient is to be sitting up and fully awake (with reversal if needed) before any extubation. Once this is stated, end scenario.

## Instructor Debrief:

In addition to your standard debrief, instructors are to:

- Emphasize the value of having backup plans and equipment ready before induction
- Emphasize gathering as much airway information as possible beforehand
